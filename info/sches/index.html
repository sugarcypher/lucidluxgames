About

Sches starts where chess does: two armies, a grid of squares, pieces that capture by reaching each other. Then it takes that apart and rebuilds it into something with no edges and no turns.

The board is a torus, a 32 by 12 grid that wraps around both ways, so there are no edges and no corners and flanking is constant. Each seat owns a coloured wedge of territory. Every player plots a move in secret; then all moves are revealed and resolved at once: collisions, captures, paths that overrun each other. Set the enemy Sun to win.

How it plays

  • The piece is its own rulebook. Each piece's shape describes how it moves. Your own pieces are transparent to you, so sliders pass through friendlies; enemies still block.
  • Sealed plots. Everyone commits in private, then the round resolves in one reveal.
  • Same-type clashes are settled by a quick duel of Connect-Four: the winner keeps the cell, the loser falls.
  • The Veil. A small hidden torus sits inside the board. Only the Sun and the Moon can rise to it.
  • Events escalate. Barriers solidify and dissolve, Sun flares and Moon beams fire on a schedule, and the endgame brings setting Suns and novas.

The pieces

  • Sun · omnidirectional, one, two or three cells
  • Moon · slides eight directions, one lap at most
  • Possibility · stealth; teleports to any empty cell
  • Day · one orthogonal cell
  • Night · one diagonal cell
  • Duality · a knight's move
  • Wizard · slides the four diagonals, one lap at most
  • Logic · slides four cells orthogonally, multi-capture
  • Ursa Minor · slides two cells diagonally, multi-capture
  • Religion · a five-cell L, multi-capture
  • Arc · a curving hook, multi-capture
  • Tree · three diagonal then a turn, multi-capture

Ways to play

Solo against the computer, Sandbox to study the board with nothing hidden, or Online. New to it? Open the Story in the game: it teaches Sches starting from chess.
